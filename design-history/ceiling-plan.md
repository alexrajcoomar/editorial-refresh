# Ceiling pass: audience, the ordering answer, and the plan

Written before any code, per the brief.

## Deliverable 1a: the audience assumption

The reader is a Canadian co-op recruiter or hiring manager in accounting,
banking or consulting, screening at volume, on a managed corporate laptop,
likely on a network that blocks third-party CDNs, giving the page thirty
seconds between two other candidates. They judge rigour, clarity and evidence
of independent thought. A second, rarer reader matters: the technical
interviewer sent the link after a first screen, who will click the Atlas and
poke it. Design for the first reader; let the second find depth without the
first paying for it. Frame rate impresses neither; a false figure would
disqualify with both.

## Deliverable 1b: the section 5 ordering answer

The order is wrong and I am rebuilding it, not defending it. The strongest
defence, that a portfolio about rigour should demonstrate rigour first, fails
on its own terms: the hero already states the rigour claim in words ("I make
arguments you can audit"), so opening with three methodology sections restates
the claim's apparatus before showing a single audited argument. That is
telling twice and showing nothing, and it is the same defect the Atlas
re-anchoring fixed at stop 01, where the highest-leverage change of that whole
pass was making the work the subject. The first screen partially survives
today only because the Most Recent rail sits beside the hero; the second
screen fails outright.

New order: hero → Start here → Selected work → the corpus figure and its named
twin → Three marks, three rules → And one that failed → A note on the
material. Nothing is deleted; the methodology moves after the work it governs,
where it reads as proof rather than preamble. The methodology sections remain
the differentiator; a differentiator is earned after substance, not instead of
it.

## Deliverable 2: the plan, by front

**A. First contact.** Reorder the home sections as above. Add the contact
address to the hero block: it exists only in the footer today, and a
thirty-second reader never reaches the footer. One computed-figure lede stays
as is. Measure home on both form factors after.

**B. Legibility.** Assess and report; act only where a fix is one commit.
Coursework at 34 of 50 documents is honestly weighted only if the pages that
present it subordinate it; I will check the two-click test on all four listing
pages and report, but I will not restructure four pages inside this pass and
say so plainly.

**C. Atlas.** One addition and two refusals. The addition: the six tool marks
leave the surface, standing off the sphere the way the shared-heading stems
already do, because "is not a heading" is a real category the data records
(the six marks with no fragment), altitude is the channel the depth pass
already established for exceptional marks, the figure is computed in
`facts()`, and the key names it. This also completes label 01's honesty
visually: the sentence already admits the six are different; the picture now
shows it. The refusals: perspective projection, again, for the standing
reason (it rewrites the label placer, hit test and depth cueing at once, on a
renderer holding every budget), and WebGL, which under the brief's own
condition 1 cannot be reached without the perspective baseline I am declining
to build. Full collision, crossing and contrast sweeps re-run because marks
move.

**D. Infrastructure.** Self-host Inter Variable, subset to the glyphs the
corpus uses, report the glyph count and byte delta, drop the CDN preload and
stylesheet reference, expect Best Practices 100 and console errors 0. Then
sweep for any other external dependency.

**E. Resilience.** Scripts off on every page; fonts blocked (fallback stack);
200% zoom; forced-colors; canvas unavailable on the Atlas; print to PDF;
keyboard walks. Report what breaks, fix what is one commit.

**Removals (section 8).** The CDN font link and preload; the dead `one`-stage
exports left in the facts blob after the re-anchoring pass; and at least one
more found during the work, reported with measurements.

**Not doing, with reasons.** No dark-first Atlas: the case is real but it
breaks site-wide coherence for the first reader on a cream site, and I will
not spend the palette's one exception on it in the same pass that moves six
marks. No home-teaser upgrade beyond what the payload cut gives: the home
page is the LCP surface and a second full-fidelity canvas above the fold is a
cost the thirty-second test never repays. No perspective, no WebGL, no glow,
no depth-of-field: each fails section 4's rule that a channel must encode a
recorded quantity, or section 8's rule that impressiveness is not a
justification.
